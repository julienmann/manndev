Local SEO is everything that helps your business appear when people nearby search for what you offer: "bakery near me", "accountant Plateau Mont-Royal", "emergency plumber Laval". For most small businesses, these searches are worth more than any other traffic, because the people making them are ready to buy.

There is a lot of noise about SEO. Most of it is not relevant to a local business. Here is what is.

How local search results work

For a local search, Google usually shows two things: a map with a short list of businesses, often called the local pack, and the regular list of websites below it. Google has said that local rankings depend mainly on three factors:

  • Relevance: how well your business matches what was searched.
  • Distance: how close you are to the searcher, or to the place they named.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted your business is, online and off. Reviews, links, mentions and your website all feed into this.

You can't move your shop closer to every customer. You can make it unmistakably clear what you do and where, and give Google every reason to trust you.

1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the single biggest lever for the map results, and it is free. Claim your profile, verify it, and fill in everything:

  • The most specific primary category that fits, plus relevant secondary ones.
  • Accurate hours, including holiday hours.
  • Your service area if you travel to customers.
  • Real photos of your work, your space and your team.
  • A link to the most relevant page on your website.

Then keep it alive. Answer questions, post updates occasionally, and add new photos. An abandoned profile signals an abandoned business.

2. Make your name, address and phone consistent everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should be written exactly the same way on your website, your Google profile, and the directories and social profiles that list you. Inconsistent details make it harder for Google to be sure all those mentions are the same business.

Put them in plain text on your website, usually in the footer and on your contact page, not only inside an image. A developer can also add structured data (schema.org markup) that states your business details in a format search engines read directly.

3. Give each service its own page

A single "Services" page with a bulleted list gives Google very little to work with. If you offer distinct services, each one deserves its own page that explains what it is, who it's for, what it costs or how pricing works, and how to get started.

The same logic applies to places. If you serve several neighbourhoods or towns, a genuinely useful page for each one can help, as long as each page has real, specific content. Copying one page and swapping the city name does more harm than good.

4. Earn reviews, and answer them

Reviews influence both your ranking and whether people click. Ask every happy customer, at the moment they're happiest, and make it easy with a direct link to your review form. Reply to every review, good or bad, calmly and specifically. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for positive ones; it breaks Google's rules and customers can tell.

5. Make the website itself easy to trust

Your Google profile gets people to your site. Your site decides whether they contact you. A few things matter most:

  • Speed on a phone. Most local searches happen on mobile, often on the go. See Core Web Vitals, explained for how to check yours.
  • Clear titles and descriptions. Each page's title should say what it is and where: "Kitchen Renovations in Montréal · Your Business" beats "Home".
  • A clear next step. A phone number you can tap, a short contact form, or a booking link on every page.
  • Accessibility. Real headings, descriptive links and alt text help screen reader users and search engines alike. The accessibility checklist covers the essentials.

6. In Québec, go bilingual properly

If you do business in Québec, the Charter of the French Language requires your commercial website to be available in French, and a French version also lets you rank for the searches many of your customers make in French. Check the Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) guidance for the details that apply to your business.

Doing it properly matters for search as well as compliance:

  • Give each language its own address, for example / and /fr/, rather than swapping text on the same page.
  • Tell Google the pages are translations of each other with hreflang tags.
  • Translate the titles, descriptions and image alt text too, not just the visible copy.
  • Write French that reads naturally for Québec, not a machine translation.

7. Publish things worth finding

Helpful content answers the questions your customers ask before they buy. A renovation company might explain permits, timelines and how to prepare for a quote. An accountant might explain tax deadlines for the self-employed. These pages bring in people who are researching, build trust, and give other sites a reason to link to you, which feeds prominence.

You don't need to publish weekly. A handful of genuinely useful, accurate guides beats a stream of thin posts written to hit a keyword.

What not to waste money on

  • Guaranteed rankings. Nobody can promise a position on Google. Anyone who does is guessing or doing something risky.
  • Bulk directory submissions and bought links. A few relevant, real listings matter. Hundreds of low-quality ones don't, and paid link schemes can get you penalised.
  • Keyword stuffing. Writing "Montréal web design" twenty times on a page makes it worse to read and no better to rank.

Where to start

If you do nothing else this month: complete your Google Business Profile, make your contact details consistent, and ask five happy customers for a review. Then look at your website with fresh eyes and ask whether a stranger would understand in five seconds what you do, where, and how to reach you.
